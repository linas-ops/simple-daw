SIMPLE DAW
==========

Start it: double-click "Start Simple DAW.bat".
The first time, it sets itself up (needs internet, about a minute).
After that it opens straight away in its own window.

Recording
1. Plug your drum module into the computer with USB and turn it on.
2. If the window asks to use MIDI devices, click Allow.
3. Hit a pad: its name lights up on the left.
4. Press R (or the red button), wait for the count-in, and play.
5. Press Space to listen back. Try other sounds with the Sound button.

While you record you hear your module, as always. Simple DAW stores
every hit as MIDI and plays it back with the sound you choose.
Every recording is kept as a new take (button at the bottom right).

Your songs save themselves in the Projects folder.
Exported WAV and MIDI files go to the Exports folder.

Realistic kits (recommended)
- Click Sound and pick a sample kit, such as "Naked Drums (studio)".
  Every hit uses a different recording, so nothing sounds repeated.
- More free kits: Sound > Get more kits... downloads them for you.
- Other SFZ kits: unzip them into the Kits folder.
- Mix > Kit mixer sets the level of each mic and drum.
- Settings > Sound variations: Random (default) or In turn (1, 2, 1, 2...)
  for how each drum picks among its recordings.
- Sample kits are heard on playback, not while you record.

Percussion
- Sound > Get more kits... > Percussion (49 MB, free): cajon, bongos,
  congas, shaker, tambourine, cowbell, agogo, triangle and more.
- Pick "Percussion" in Sound and your pads play it: kick = cajon bass,
  snare = cajon slap, toms = bongos and congas, hi-hat = shaker,
  ride = cowbell, crash = triangle.
- With any other kit, percussion lanes (conga, shaker...) play from
  Percussion automatically.

Each drum: right-click its name (or click its ... button)
- Move hits to another drum: e.g. turn the kick part into a snare part.
- Copy hits to another drum: both drums play them (e.g. snare + conga).
- Swap with another drum: each drum gets the other's hits.
  Ctrl+Z undoes any of these. Select hits first to move only those.
- Choose another sound: give this drum a sound from any kit you have,
  like a tom from another kit or a bongo from Percussion.
- Variation: sample kits record each drum several times. "Any" uses
  them all; a number makes this drum always play that one recording.
  The drum name then shows v and the number (e.g. v4).
- Volume: make this drum louder or softer (also in Mix).

My kits (save your kit setup)
- Sound > Save kit setup... saves the kit with its borrowed sounds,
  chosen variations, drum volumes and kit mixer (or built-in kit
  balance). Pick it in any song from Sound > My kits.
- Saving again with the same name updates it.
- The Sound button shows "My kit, changed" when this song's kit
  differs from the saved one. Hits and mutes stay with each song.
- Lanes menu (top left) > Show percussion lanes to see them all.

Plugins
- 64-bit VST3 and VST2 plugins show up under Sound > Plugins.
- Some drum plugins (MT Power Drum Kit, Steven Slate Drums) stay
  silent in Simple DAW. Use a sample kit instead.

If something goes wrong
- "No drum module found": close other music programs that might be using
  it (Cakewalk, Audacity), then unplug and replug the USB cable.
- Hits land late compared to the click: Settings > Timing correction.
- Lanes have the wrong names: Settings > Note map (Roland or standard).
- To see a log window, run app\Start with log window.bat.
  The log file is app\data\simple-daw.log.
